# CashAhead | Three-minute video and judge walkthrough

Start on the [CashAhead camera landing page](https://cashahead-ps1.vercel.app/). A judge can reproduce the entire video in one browser tab with the sample data already in the app. To run it on your own computer, see the local testing instructions at the end.

**For the presenter:** show the app throughout. Read the blockquotes aloud. The bold *On-screen caption* lines are short video overlays you can add in editing. The clicks are your directions. Use a desktop browser, keep the PDF and the app in the recording area, and begin with the camera cover at the top of the page.

**For the judge:** follow the clicks in order. **Try the judge demo** starts a fresh session on Monday, 23 November 2026. Opening that entry again resets the example. All figures below are in Indian rupees.

## 0:00–0:13 | Open on the camera

**Click** — Hold on the camera, crew and **Know your Friday.** for two seconds. Click **Try the judge demo** in the top-right corner. Hold on **₹4,000 short on Fri, 27 Nov**.

> Ananya shoots weddings in Vijayawada. Her clients can pay late. Her crew still needs paying on Friday. Let’s open her studio.

## 0:13–0:31 | Show who owes what

**On-screen caption — PS1 required · Who owes me, how much, and since when?**

**Click** — **Overview** in the sidebar. Point to **Cash received & on hand**, **Friday closing balance**, then the two names under **Overdue invoices**.

> Six thousand in hand, ten thousand in Friday bills. Kiran and Divya owe eight thousand, twelve days overdue. Sai Teja owes thirty-five hundred, five days overdue. Both due dates are right here.

## 0:31–0:55 | Change the timing of a real invoice

**On-screen captions — PS1 required · 30–90 day cash forecast** when the chart appears. **PS1 required · What if a client pays late?** when the dialog opens.

**Click** — **Forecast**. Point to **30d / 60d / 90d**, then click **90d**. Click **What if a client pays late?** Select **INV-104 · Reddy–Varma**. Under **Days after contractual due date**, replace `15` with `0`, pause on **−₹4,000 → +₹7,000**, then enter `15` again. Click **Cancel preview**.

> This is the ninety-day view of her invoices and bills. The forecast assumes Reddy–Varma will pay fifteen days late. If it comes on time, Friday closes seven thousand positive. Restore the delay and she’s four thousand short.

## 0:55–1:15 | Turn the shortfall alert into a decision

**On-screen captions — PS1 required · Alert leads to an action** on the Overview alert. **Extra · Collection ranking changes with the amount** on the toggle.

**Click** — **Overview**. Scroll to **Next action** and click **Review rescue plan →**. Show Kiran & Divya at **50%**. Click **25%**, point to **₹2,000 still needed** and the new recommendation, then return to **50%**.

> The alert tells Ananya whom to call. Half of Kiran and Divya’s balance covers Friday. At a quarter, she’s still two thousand short. The first call switches to Lakshmi and Arjun for an early deposit.

## 1:15–1:35 | Create the invoice the judge will see again

**On-screen caption — PS1 required · Create an invoice | Mira Shah, INV-107**

**Click** — **Invoices → + New invoice**. Enter **Client** `Mira Shah`, **Shoot or service** `Portrait`, **Invoice amount** `9000`, and **Contractual due date** **30 November 2026**. Leave **Invoice type** at **Final balance** and **Earliest possible receipt** empty. Click **Save invoice**. Click **Mira Shah** in the invoice list.

> Now Mira books a portrait session. I’ll create her nine-thousand-rupee invoice, due Monday. Watch invoice one-oh-seven. We’ll follow this same record all the way to the client’s copy.

## 1:35–2:02 | Negotiate the smallest split that works

**On-screen caption — Extra · Last safe payment date and smallest workable split**

**Click** — In Mira’s invoice, click **Test a client offer**. Leave **First payment date** at **Fri 27 Nov** and **Balance payment date** at **Sat 28 Nov**. Type `3000` in **First payment (₹)**. Point to the ₹1,000 shortfall and **Last safe date for full payment**. Click **Use smallest split**, pause on **Bills covered**, then click **Save agreed promise**.

> Mira offers three thousand Friday and the rest Saturday. That leaves Friday a thousand short. Friday is the last safe date for full payment. For a split, four thousand Friday and five thousand Saturday covers the bills. I’ll save that agreement. Cash is still six thousand.

## 2:02–2:25 | Record a partial payment and draft the follow-up

**On-screen caption — PS1 required · Track payment and draft a follow-up**

**Click** — **Invoices → Mira Shah → Record payment**. Enter `1000` in **Amount received**. Keep **Received date** at **23 November** and click **Confirm payment**. Reopen **Mira Shah → Draft reminder**. Pause on the message and the **WhatsApp draft / Gmail draft** options. Close the dialog with **×**.

> A thousand arrives early. I’ll record it once. Mira’s reminder now asks for three thousand Friday and five thousand Saturday. Ananya can edit the message before opening WhatsApp or Gmail.

## 2:25–2:42 | Open the actual client PDF

**On-screen caption — Extra · Client PDF reconciles the receipt and both agreed dates**

**Click** — Reopen **Mira Shah → Download PDF**. In the browser’s downloads list, click the newest **CashAhead-INV-107.pdf** to open it. If the list is hidden, press **Ctrl+J**. Point to **Remaining balance**, the single row in **Payment history**, then the **Still due** column under **Agreed payment dates**. Return to the CashAhead tab and close the invoice dialog.

> Here’s Mira’s PDF. Nine thousand invoiced, one thousand received, eight thousand left. The original due date stays November thirtieth. Each agreed installment shows what’s still due.

## 2:42–3:00 | Price the next booking

**On-screen caption — Extra · Minimum deposit protects the next booking**

**Click** — **Booking deposit → Try worked example**. Point to **₹7,000**. Change **Cost amount** from `10000` to `12000`. Finish on **₹9,000** minimum deposit and **₹21,000** final balance.

> For the next thirty-thousand-rupee job, Ananya wants a three-thousand-rupee buffer. The minimum deposit is seven thousand. Raise costs by two thousand and it becomes nine thousand. That’s the advance she can quote.

**Final arithmetic.** Mira’s ₹9,000 invoice less ₹1,000 received leaves **₹8,000**. The PDF splits that balance into **₹3,000** still due Friday and **₹5,000** still due Saturday. Back on **Overview** after her receipt, cash is ₹7,000 and Friday closes at ₹0. The booking calculator’s **worked example** uses its own ₹6,000 starting cash and ₹3,000 buffer.

### Run the tests locally

From the GitHub checkout, use Node.js 20 or newer. The browser suite uses installed Chromium or Microsoft Edge. If needed, install the project’s Chromium build with `npx playwright-core install chromium`.

```sh
npm ci
npm test
npm run build
npm run test:demo
npm start
```

Open the local homepage and start at 0:00. The browser suite also exercises the mobile layout, camera tour, bank reconciliation, saved promises, PDF downloads and sharing fallbacks. [Open CashAhead locally](http://127.0.0.1:4317/)
